Status: done with concerns
Board commit: edace7d (main, "board: s1, the Meadow sketch, building")

## What it is

One self-contained page (plain JS and a canvas, about 1,800 lines; Geist Mono from Google Fonts is its only outside
resource). The top-down Meadow runs live at fixed 60 Hz steps on the app's cell grid: 13 × 19 pt cells (12 × 17
at 1440 wide, by the app's own `GridSpec.cell(forWidth:)`), each cell a block colour plus one glyph in Geist Mono
SemiBold at `round(ch × 0.74)` px, with the faint grid lines. It uses only glyphs already in `GlyphAtlas`. The
panel on the right holds the sliders; the `H` key or the Tweaks button hides it, and Space pauses.

How it answers the director's notes (the Corrections are the acceptance test):
- **From above, with depth.** Each butterfly has a height. Drawn size grows with it, from "size down on the
  flowers" to "size up close". Its shadow falls down-right of it (the light is top-left), drawn at its ground
  size, fainter and softer the higher it flies. Nearer butterflies draw over farther ones.
- **Few, spread over the whole screen, always moving.** 4 by default, one of each species. Each new waypoint is
  the best of 9 random points for being far away, away from the other butterflies and their goals, and roughly
  ahead. Neighbours steer apart, and they are pushed apart if they touch. Measured over 2 minutes (seed 5): the
  four visited all 40 regions of a 180 pt grid over a 1440 × 900 screen, and a pair was within 150 pt only 1.9 %
  of the time. Over 3 minutes (seed 8): 76 % of the time flying (46 % travelling, 30 % heading to a flower or a
  basking spot), 13 % on flowers, 11 % basking; the body was off screen 2.6 % of the time.
- **They move like butterflies.** This ports the measured per-beat model (Butterfly.swift), turned on its side as
  p2a's notes planned. Each downstroke gives a forward push and drag pulls the pace back between pushes, so the
  ground speed pulses every beat: measured 110 ↔ 165 pt/s for the morpho and 67 ↔ 140 pt/s for the white.
  Each wingbeat can also bring a jink (a kick to the heading target, on the species' full heading spring, so it
  is a flick), a sideways slip, the white's surge and the morpho's bounds. Flap bouts alternate with glides:
  wings held flat, the pace eases off and it sinks toward the flowers, which is flap-and-glide. The bob is the
  rise and fall within each beat (lift on the downstroke against gravity), shown as a pulse in size and in the
  shadow.
- **Faster wingbeat; both wings together.** The default is 0.5 × real (monarch 4.8 Hz, white 5.9 Hz, morpho
  3.0 Hz; the app today is 0.28 × capped at 3 Hz). The cap is 7.5 Hz, so a beat never takes fewer than 4 frames
  at 30 fps. Both wings share one stroke. Seen from above, a wing raised by φ is drawn cos φ wide, never under
  0.1. Turning banks them slightly differently, by at most 35 % of the species' bank.
- **Turn to face where they fly.** The whole butterfly is drawn rotated to its heading, all the way round, with a
  slight yaw on each beat.
- **Shaded, detailed wings; stylised colours.** Each species' outline and markings from MeadowPainter are drawn
  from above: the monarch's black veins as dark line glyphs on the orange, its black border with white dots and
  pale apex spots; the morpho's black forewing margin with dots; the swallowtail's black bars, blue lunules, red
  eyespot and tails; the white's black tips and spots. Each field is a four-step colour ramp. It is lit by each
  wing's own tilt to the light, so the two wings differ and pulse as they beat. It is deeper at the root and has
  a darker rim. Every cell carries a texture glyph keyed to its place on the wing, so the texture travels with
  the wing as it turns (the reference's mosaic idea, our own glyphs). The morpho's blue flashes paler where a
  wing's tilt catches the light. As the wings close toward the clap, the underside colour shows.
- **Mainly black with hints of green.** The ground is black with slow, very dark green patches. Grass marks
  brighten and lie over along the wind as gust bands roll across the screen. Leaves are dark green rosettes.
- **Much better flowers.** Seven kinds seen from above, in patches across the whole screen. Daisies,
  coneflowers and dandelions are drawn as ASCII starbursts: each petal is a dim block carrying a bright stroke
  that points along it (`\|/ -o- /|\`), around a lit disc or cone. Milkweed is a lit dome of florets, and fennel a
  lacy umbel of fine rays and dotted umbellets. Clover heads sit among three-leaf leaves, and mustard is a scatter
  of little crosses. Each head sways on a damped spring driven by the wind (research §4.2), casts a small shadow
  and has its own leaves. A click blooms a new flower that grows in with a pollen sparkle; a passing butterfly
  may come to it. Butterflies leaving a flower shed pollen motes that drift on the breeze.
- **Behaviour (simple wants).** Travel to a waypoint; on arrival, maybe visit a flower (weighted by the
  species' favourites) or bask on the ground; then move on. On a flower the wings open and close slowly (the
  swallowtail keeps fluttering). Basking: the monarch and swallowtail open flat, the white holds a V, and the
  morpho stays closed with a flash open now and then. A fast pointer stirs the air and flushes nearby butterflies.

## What reads well, what doesn't

Reads well: the starburst daisies, coneflowers and dandelions; milkweed; the swallowtail and morpho wings (colour,
markings, light on the wings); the beat (open → blade → open, both wings together); the jerky beat-by-beat pace;
flap-and-glide; turning to face the way; spread over the screen; the black ground with green hints; the light
theme's shadows.

Concerns:
- **Shadows barely show on black.** On a black ground there is little to darken. They show over flowers, leaves,
  the green patches and the grass marks, and clearly in the light theme. In dark, depth comes mostly from size.
  The "Green in the ground" and "Shadows" sliders help; a stylised shadow (for example a dim glyph texture) is a
  director call.
- **Small butterflies lose their markings.** At ground size the white and the monarch get coarse (11–16 cells
  across); the monarch's veins break up. The size sliders decide where the line is.
- **The clap is drawn as a narrow, darker blade** in the underside colour; at 5 Hz it reads as a flicker. Whether
  it should keep more width is a look call.
- **Fennel, mustard and clover are weaker than the big blooms.** Fennel is airy and faint; mustard and clover
  read mostly as texture. They are weighted down so the big blooms lead.
- **The light theme is a first pass.** It is readable and its shadows are good, but the flowers are pale and the
  black marks heavy.
- **Coneflower cones and the morpho's highlights are busy.** They are glyph-heavy, which may be louder than the
  calm desktop the plan asks for.
- **The bob pulse is subtle** at 1 × (a few % of size). At 2–3 × it shows clearly but starts to look like
  jitter.

## Rulings

- Grid size follows the app's formula (12 × 17 pt at 1440 wide), not a fixed 13 × 19, so the sketch looks as the
  app would on the same screen. If wrong: sizes in cells shift by 8 %.
- Glyphs limited to the existing `GlyphAtlas` set (no `=` like the reference), so the port needs no atlas change.
  If wrong: a few texture glyphs could be appended later.
- Butterflies stay apart at any height (seen from above, two over each other read as one muddle), which goes
  further than the direction note's "spacing among butterflies at similar heights". If wrong: occasional overlaps
  at different heights would come back.
- The stroke runs from about 80° up to about −15°, so seen from above a wing goes open → blade → open once per
  beat, not twice. If wrong: the side view's full downstroke would show the wings widest twice a beat.
- In the light theme, flowers are drawn in the dark palette and mapped onto the paper per cell. Butterflies keep
  their own light palettes.
- The sketch has two development aids in its URL: `?sheet=flowers` / `?sheet=flies` (close-up sheets) and
  `?warm=S&freeze=1&seed=N` (a still after S seconds). `?debug=1` exposes the stepper for scripted checks. They
  are harmless and help the port's comparison stills.

## Noticed

- The app's checks for flight (bob in rows, glide angle, bottom-row coverage) belong to the side view; the port
  needs new ones for height, the size pulse and screen coverage (as the direction note says).
- The cell size makes ground-level butterflies coarse on a laptop (12 × 17 pt cells); the size sliders, not the
  painter, are the lever.

# Round 2: the Meadow sketch looks like the family

Status: done with concerns
Board commit: 107a0e9 (main, "board: family-details research saved; s1 round 2 building"; adds briefs/s1r2.md)
Commit: the commit that adds this section on `meadow-sketch` (from 0e2ecf7)

## What changed, in the brief's order

1. **The mosaic.** The page now draws as the app's cell canvas does (Shaders.swift `canvas_main`): the ground first,
   seamless (it is the habitat's background), then the grid lines, then every block **inset one point** from its
   cell's top-left lines, then the glyphs. Grid lines start at row and column 0. Wings, flowers, leaves and eyes all
   sit in their own tiles with a strip of ground and grid showing.
2. **Edges like the family.** Wings and flower heads use the family's five samples (the centre, then (0.25, 0.3),
   (0.75, 0.3), (0.25, 0.72), (0.75, 0.72)). A cell is a block only when the centre is covered and at least 3 of 5
   are; otherwise it drops the block and shows a light glyph, `: ;` from half covered, else `. ' , \``, at
   (70 + 80 × coverage) × dim, tinted by the wing's (or petal's) hue. The old "rim" cells with a block are gone.
3. **Carved dark glyphs on lit blocks.** Wing field glyphs come from the skin ramp `: ; t % S X 8 # @` by
   (1 − shade) × 8.99 ± 1 step from the key, in Ocean's carved ink ((14,15,19) on lit, (27,29,36) in shade). The
   field colour is the species ramp read continuously, not in four steps. No bare cells, except three in ten on the
   cabbage white. Black marks keep `# % @ 8 X`, now on every mark cell, in a dim ink (about 70, like the sharks' bars).
   Glyphs are keyed to the place on the wing's surface (its full span, not the drawn width), so they ride with the
   wing through the beat instead of re-rolling as the wing narrows.
4. **The hide shimmer.** Every cell re-rolls 2.4 times a second on its own phase (Ocean's clock,
   floor(t × 2.4 + (key mod 997) / 97)). Wing field and body cells: 6 % one of the seven family accents (by key
   mod 7), 4 % a white fleck (a near-black one on the cabbage white, which shows in its bare cells); field edge
   cells 7 % accents. Not on marks, spots, rings, eyespots or veins. Flowers' petals and discs at half the rate
   (3 % / 2 %). A new **Sparkle** slider (Look group) scales it all; Calm × 0.35. On paper: no accents, flecks
   in the paper colour, as Web.
5. **Depth in brightness.** A butterfly is drawn at 0.78 + 0.28 × height: 0.78 on a flower, 1.06 at the glass.
6. **Small anatomy.** Butterflies drawn 15 cells across or more get two eyes beside the head: the family's eye, a
   dark block (level 12) with `o` in the eye ink (154,160,173), the paper colour on paper; the head cell becomes
   `%`. Landed with wings closed or in a vee (stroke above 45°), three legs a side as Web draws legs: stroke glyphs
   on dim blocks (glyph l × 1.12, block l × 0.34), only where no wing block covers them. The front pair reach
   forward past the forewings, so they show in a vee; all six show with closed wings.
7. **Thin lines like silk.** Web's `lineGlyph` (`¯ - _` by where a level line runs in its cell, `|` beyond
   1.12 rad, else `/ \`) for veins, petal strokes, fennel rays, leaf ribs, legs, antennae, the abdomen and lying
   grass. Veins and fennel rays are drawn as Web draws silk: one glyph per step along the major axis, so they are
   unbroken at any angle. A small wing keeps one vein per wing; more appear on wings over 10 and 14 cells (half
   span), because an unbroken line takes a whole cell per step.
8. **Light mode, the family way.** Everything is drawn in the dark palette and each kind of cell maps to paper as
   Web's painter does (`Palette`): bodies (wings, the body, discs) by Web's `tone` (the level runs from the ink to
   the paper, the hue at half strength), carved glyphs in Web's paper inks ((35,35,35) / (22,22,22)), petals,
   florets, leaves and legs as Web's legs (a dim block under a bright stroke becomes a pale block under a dark
   stroke), grass, rays and antennae as silk (the ink, 0.85 × alpha), and no accents. The ground is the paper with
   hints of green. The old separate light palettes (`onPaper`, `paperTint`, per-species light ramps) are gone.
9. **A reset beside every control.** Each slider, choice, toggle and the species chips has a `↺` button beside it,
   dimmed while that control is at its default, with the default in its label. "Reset" became "Reset all".
10. **Second priority, done.** Gold `·` motes where the pointer stirs the air above 60 pt/s, up to 0.35 alpha, in
   empty cells, fading as the stirred air dies away. A flower a butterfly leaves after nectaring dims its disc
   × 0.85 for 20–40 s and its glyphs drop a step (`o → ° → ·`, `* → x → +`), easing back over the last third.

## What I saw side by side with Ocean and Web

Ocean and Web stills were rendered with `OceanTool still … 1440x900 --habitat ocean|web --seed 3 --warmup 10 --live`,
dark and `--light`, and compared with the sketch at the same size, whole and in crops (the sketch's monarch next to
a shark and a spider, dark and light).

- **Dark: it is the same family now.** The tiles and gaps, the carved dark skin glyphs (`: ; t` on the lit blocks,
  `8 # @` in the shade), the glyph-only edges and the odd coloured fleck read the same in all three. The morpho and
  the cabbage white are the closest match; the white looks like a pale shark flank.
- **Light: graphite on paper, as Web.** Wings become pale-to-mid tiles with the hue at half strength (the monarch a
  warm tan, the morpho a grey blue), carved glyphs dark, black marks dark graphite (Web's spiders' marks do the
  same). Flowers become pale tiles with ink strokes, like Web's legs. The Ocean light still looks much the same
  (mid-grey bodies, dark carving), except that Ocean keeps its accents on paper; I followed Web and the brief (off).
- **Depth:** the same butterfly drawn on the flowers and up close differs clearly in brightness as well as size.
- Calm dims toward black, or toward the paper on paper, and cuts the shimmer to 0.35.

## What still differs

- **The monarch is busier than a shark.** Its veins are unbroken lines and its border carries mark glyphs on
  every cell, so it has more line glyphs than any shark or spider skin. At the default sizes it shows two veins
  each side. Fewer veins, or veins only above a size, is a director call.
- **Flowers keep their own hand.** Petals are dim tiles with bright strokes (starbursts), not lit tiles with
  carved glyphs; only the tile, the edges, the shimmer and the paper mapping follow the family. I kept the
  starbursts because round 1's flowers were liked; the brief asks for carved glyphs on the wings.
- **The ground is the meadow's own.** The family's background is flat black or paper; ours keeps the round-1 green
  hints and grass marks, which the director asked for ("mainly black with hints of green").
- **Light leaves** are a pale mint (the hue at 0.16 strength); still the most coloured thing on the paper.

## Rulings

- **The shimmer's clock is Ocean's, not Web's.** Web's formula truncates its offset to whole ticks
  (`hash3(key, int(tick + (key mod 97) / 41), 7)`), so cells re-roll in three groups, not on their own phase;
  the brief asks for staggered phases, which Ocean has. If wrong: the flicker comes in three beats a tick.
- **On paper, flecks stay, in the paper colour,** because that is what Web's code does (its light accent list is
  empty, so 10 % of cells take the paper-coloured fleck). If "off" meant no flecks either, it is one line.
- **Paper mapping by kind of cell**, following Web's two rules (bodies by `tone`, legs inverted). Petals, florets
  and leaves count as legs (dim tiles under bright strokes); the daisy and dandelion discs count as bodies; the
  coneflower's dark cone counts as a stroke tile, so its bright glyphs become ink. Pale stroke tiles carry their
  hue at 0.3 (leaves 0.16) instead of Web's 0.5, which turned leaves mint and petals loud at a pale level.
- **The ground is the background**, not blocks: the 1 pt gap shows the ground (green hints) under each tile, so
  the green patches stay seamless and only things on the ground are tiled.
- **Edge colours by region:** a field edge takes the wing's hue; a mark's edge a dim neutral (0.6 × the level), so
  a black border doesn't get a coloured halo.
- **Edge cells over a flower keep the flower's tile under the glyph** (a wing edge does not punch a hole).
- **The shine glints on the morpho** (round 1's `* ·` sparkles) were dropped; the lit tile carries the shine, and
  the shimmer gives the sparkle.

## Noticed

- Ocean's light theme keeps its accents on paper while Web's does not; the family is not consistent there. The
  Meadow follows Web.
- The vein density rule (one cell per step) will matter for the port: the Swift painter can reuse
  `WebPainter`'s `lineGlyph` and stroke accumulator directly.
- The panel's long labels wrap beside their values (for example "Wingbeat speed"); harmless.

# Round 3: the Meadow sketch moves and lives like the family

Status: done with concerns
Board commit: df9f204 (main, "board: the director's sketch settings; s1 round 3 (motion and behaviour) briefed"; adds briefs/s1r3.md)
Commit: the commit that adds this section on `meadow-sketch` (from d893c50)
Files changed: `Tools/meadow-sketch/meadow.html`, `reports/s1.md`. Nothing in `Sources/` changed.

## What changed, in the brief's order

1. **The director's settings are the defaults**: count 7, lurch 2, zig-zag 1.29, glide 0.44, speed 2, turning 1.23,
   bask 0.2, density 0.85, flower size 1.32, leaves 1.24, breeze 1.7, green 0.69 (the rest were already theirs).
   Every ↺ and "Reset all" go back to them. The lurch slider now reaches 3 (speed 3). The count stays a plain count;
   a new switch beside it, **Scale the count by screen area** (off), reads it as "per 1440 × 900 screen". Saved
   values moved to a new storage key, so round 2's saved values don't override the new defaults.
2. **Nothing snaps.** Take-off no longer sets the heading: it kicks the turn rate and the heading spring swings the
   body round. The beat starts where it matches the landed pose: wings held high (closed, a vee) go straight into the
   downstroke, wings held flat first rise. Anything left over blends in over 0.15 s. Landing: over the last beats the
   push drops to 0.3 and the stroke to half (the brake), the airspeed brakes away instead of zeroing, and the wings
   ease into the landed pose over 0.35 s. The end of a glide blends the same way. Slider: **Soft take-off and
   landing** (0 is round 2's snap).
3. **In and out through edges.** Raising the count brings newcomers in from a real edge, just out of sight. Each
   takes the best of 6 entry points for room from the others, then a waypoint, and they trickle in one every
   0.4–0.9 s. Lowering the count (or turning a species off) sends the spare ones (flying ones nearest an edge
   first) out through the nearest edge; they are removed once out of sight. Raising it again turns leavers back
   before anyone new comes. **Opening moment:** one nectaring, one basking (both part-way through their stay), one
   about to land on a flower 3–4.5 wingspans away, the rest flying. Starting places are spread (best of 12 for
   room), so they don't start in a heap.
4. **Hold still and one lands on the pointer.** Hold the pointer still (within 2.5 pt) for 3 s. One flying
   butterfly is chosen, weighted swallowtail and white 2, monarch 1, morpho 0.5, and by nearness. It flies to the
   pointer, spirals in over 1.5–2.2 shrinking circles (about 3–4 s) while rising, and sits **up at the glass**
   (height 0.92, drawn large, its shadow far below) with its proboscis at the pointer. It holds its wings in a vee and
   opens them flat and closes again every 4–10 s. Any move over 2.5 pt sends it off in an escape burst (× 2 speed,
   beat × 1.25, 0.6–1.2 s, away from the pointer). It leaves on its own after 25–45 s, or when the pointer leaves the
   meadow. Controls: **Land on a still pointer** (on), **Still for** (3 s).
5. **Senses, not knows; the air carries the pointer.** Flowers are sensed only within 12 wingspans and not behind
   (cos ≥ −0.2), and never right beside one already taken. The pointer now only stirs the air. A landed butterfly
   reads the air in its cell (breeze and stir alike, not other wings' downwash). Above **Wings close in air of**
   (60 pt/s) it leans downwind and starts closing its wings; above **Flushed by air of** (120 pt/s), both × its
   boldness, it takes off 0.05–0.15 s later, away from the flow. A flying one in strongly stirred air veers off
   across the flow and beats harder. **Alarm to neighbours** (0.6): a flushed one startles landed ones within 3
   wingspans after 0.15–0.4 s, timid ones likelier, passed on once more at half the chance. **Touch-and-go** (0.25):
   a landing broken off at the last moment rises, circles the bloom once at about a wingspan, then tries again; a
   second break-off sends it to another flower.
6. **Wants and temperament.** **Hunger**: nectar runs down 1 %/s in flight and fills while nectaring. Visit chance
   is × (0.2 + 1.6 × hunger), and a hungry one (nectar < 0.3) turns aside for a flower it senses. **Warmth**: after
   60 s in the air it cools; bask chance is × (0.45 + 1.4 × cold), and basking warms it. **Boldness** 0.6–1.4 each
   (spread slider): it scales the air a butterfly sits through, the stay, and its height (± 0.15). **Spacing only at
   similar heights** (on): full spacing within Δz 0.15–0.35. Across heights there is still a softer steer and a light
   push, so a high one crosses over a low one but doesn't linger stacked (see Rulings).
7. **Small life when landed** (**Life when landed**, 1): the abdomen breathes (1 + 0.05 sin 1.6 t), the wings
   breathe ± 1.5°. After 1.5 s still an antenna twitches out and back now and then (0.3 s, eased). A ± 0.15 rad
   shuffle every 4–9 s. A lean downwind and a part-close in a gust. The proboscis shows while drinking.

## What happens now when you keep clicking the same point

The first click on open ground blooms a flower. Every click after that lands on that bloom and **touches** it: the
head nods away from your click on its spring, a small puff of gold pollen sparks comes off it (8 sparks, 3 if you
click again within 0.5 s), and a butterfly sitting on it takes off away from your click, through the normal soft
take-off with an escape burst. Nothing piles up. After 30 clicks, fast (every 0.1 s) or slow (every 1.5 s), there is
still one flower there, and at most one butterfly was called to it.

## 1. Paused species toggles no longer grow the array

The cause, as the re-check said: at a full count the turn-back of leavers ran before the spare ones of an
over-share species were sent out, so turning Morpho back on found no room, sent three monarchs out and made three
new morphos while the three old morphos stayed as leavers. Paused, nothing ever drained them.

What changed, all in `syncFlies`:
- The spare ones of an over-share species are sent out first, then leavers of a species short of its share turn
  back, then the fill.
- After load, the fill takes a species short of its share first, so it lands on the mix fix round 1 defined.
- Never a new butterfly of a species while one of its leavers is still here: that leaver turns back instead. So no
  species ever holds more than the count.

## 2. The replay fixture
