## How the proof works

The wallpaper decisions lived in `SystemWallpaper` (the app target), which the checks can't reach. The proof commit
moves them unchanged into OceanKit's `WallpaperKeeper` (`Sources/OceanKit/Domain/WallpaperMemory.swift`), behind a
`DesktopImages` protocol: the attached displays, read and set the picture on a display's Space in view, list and
remove stills. `SystemWallpaper` becomes its host: NSWorkspace on the screens, the stills folder, the renderer, the
debounce and the generation. The checks (`Sources/OceanChecks/WallpaperChecks.swift`, `wallpaperKeeperChecks()`)
drive it through `FakeDesktop`: displays with several Spaces, one in view, that keep their pictures when unplugged and
plugged back, and reads, sets and removes that fail on demand (a set can also say yes and change nothing). Nothing
in the checks touches the real desktop or disk.

## Causes and fixes

1. **The original is recorded once and never overwritten by our own still.** Cause: one original per display, and
   a successful restore forgot it. With a still on two Spaces, the quit on Space 2 forgot the original; the still
   left on Space 1 was then replaced by a new still with nothing recorded under it, and every later restore found
   no target. Fix: each still is recorded before it goes up with the picture it covers (`WallpaperMemory.stills`,
   `StillRecord`); a still put up over our own still inherits what that one covered, never the still. A restore
   keeps the display's original and marks it restored; a different picture seen there afterwards is taken as the
   user's new choice (what the old "forget" was for), while stills up elsewhere keep their own record.
2. **A still in use is never deleted.** Cause: pruning kept the newest three stills per display by filename,
   blind to the stills other Spaces showed. Fix: pruning deletes only this display's stills that nothing shows:
   not recorded as up, not still rendering, not the new one. A still's record goes when it is replaced on its
   Space or put back (then its file is deleted). Stills on disk under a memory written before records (3.0, or a
   lost memory) are adopted as up, never deleted.
3. **Recovery material stays until a restore is verified; uninstall keeps it and says so.** Cause: a set or a
   restore counted when it didn't throw; a failure on set still reached pruning; uninstall deleted the stills
   folder and the defaults domain (with the memory) whatever the restore did, and the GUI dropped its problems.
   Fix: a set and a restore count only when the picture reads back; a still that didn't go up is dropped, one that
   can't be read back is kept with its record. `RestoreOutcome` says which displays were put back, which failed or
   couldn't be read (a display we changed before), how many stills are still up elsewhere and which of the user's
   pictures aren't back. When anything isn't verified back (or the restore couldn't be tried), uninstall keeps the
   Stills folder and writes the memory back after removing the defaults domain, and says so: the GUI shows an alert
   with what it couldn't do, the CLI prints it. The words: "Your own wallpaper couldn't be put back everywhere: a
   display that failed or isn't connected now, or a Space not in view, may still show ASCII Animals' picture. Its
   pictures are kept in ~/Library/Application Support/ASCII Animals/Stills, so nothing goes blank. Choose your
   wallpaper in System Settings > Wallpaper (yours was A.heic), then you can delete that folder."
4. **A display that comes back gets its picture back.** Cause: restores saw only attached screens, and with
   matching off nothing ever looked again; quit restored only with matching on. Fix: with matching off the app
   takes our still down wherever it shows (`WallpaperKeeper.reconcile`) at launch (a restart after a kill), when
   displays change, on a Space switch and on wake. Quit restores whatever the setting. With matching on, a display
   that comes back gets a fresh still that inherits the record, and quit puts the picture back.
5. **Still filenames are unique.** Cause: `still-<display>-<milliseconds>.png`: a burst reused names (overwriting
   a still in use), and a clock set back broke newest-by-name pruning. Fix: `still-<display>-<UUID>.png`; pruning
   no longer depends on order.

The menu's Restore Original Wallpaper restores once (by turning matching off, which restores), or tries again when
matching is already off. Files: `WallpaperMemory.swift`, `SystemWallpaper.swift`, `Uninstaller.swift`, the
wallpaper, quit and uninstall entry points in `main.swift`, the Restore item in `StatusMenu.swift`,
`WallpaperChecks.swift` and one line in `OceanChecks/main.swift`.

## What the public APIs can't guarantee

- **A Space not in view.** macOS gives an app no Space identity and reads or sets only the Space in view on each
  display. A still on a Space the person doesn't visit after quitting stays until they visit it with ASCII Animals
  open (with matching off it comes down on the switch; with it on it is replaced by a fresh still, which the next
  quit puts back). So the app changes less: a still goes only on the Space in view when a look changes, a Space
  switch never adds one, and with matching off a switch only takes ours down. Uninstall can't reach those Spaces,
  so it keeps the stills (nothing goes blank) and says so.
- **A still's record can outlive it.** If the person changes the picture on a Space our still was on, or deletes
  that Space, its record stays; uninstall then keeps the folder and says "may". Cautious, not harmful.
- **Read-back checks the URL only.** Options (scaling, fill) aren't compared, and how dynamic and native wallpapers
  round-trip their URL needs the live check below; so does whether "Show on all Spaces" makes one set change every
  Space.
- **A display that comes back after uninstall** shows the kept still until the person picks their wallpaper.

Settings help text (words to the manager for the director), under "Match system wallpaper":
"Your own wallpaper comes back when you quit, turn this off or uninstall. macOS lets an app change only the Space
you're on, so another Space gets yours back the next time you visit it with ASCII Animals open."

## Live proof (a separate macOS account, never the owner's)

For the manager to run with the director. Setup: a separate macOS user account with this build installed in that
account's own Applications; two displays (one external); "Displays have separate Spaces" on; two Spaces on the
built-in display. Pick picture A on every Space of the built-in display and B on the external. Between steps,
collect the app's log lines starting `restore:`, a listing of that account's Stills folder, and
`defaults read <bundle id> wallpaperMemory.v1` in that account.

1. **A second restore keeps the original.** Matching on. On Space 1 change a look (a still comes up); switch to
   Space 2, change a look. Quit (on Space 2): A on Space 2. Open the app, switch to Space 1 (still showing the old
   still), change a look, quit: A on Space 1. Visit Space 2: A.
2. **A still in use is never deleted.** Make four Spaces; change a look on each so each shows a still; then change
   looks three more times on Space 1. Every Space still shows a habitat picture (none grey or blank) and the Stills
   folder has one file per Space. Quit, visit each Space with the app open and matching off: A everywhere, and the
   folder empties as each comes back.
3. **Recovery stays until verified; uninstall says so.** With stills on both displays, unplug the external and
   uninstall from the menu: the built-in display gets A; the alert says the wallpaper couldn't be put back
   everywhere and names the Stills folder and B; the Stills folder and the memory key remain. Plug the external
   back: the kept still shows (not blank). Pick B in System Settings, delete the folder.
4. **A display that comes back gets its picture back.** Reinstall. Matching on, a still on both displays. Unplug the
   external; turn matching off (the built-in gets A); plug it back: B within a moment. Restart after a kill: matching
   on, a still on Spaces 1 and 2; on Space 1 turn matching off (Space 1 gets A, Space 2 keeps its still); kill the
   app (`kill -9`), open it again and visit Space 2: A.
5. **Unique names.** Drag a look slider for a few seconds with matching on: every still in the folder has a UUID
   name, and the one showing is never overwritten. Set the clock back an hour and change a look: the new still goes
   up and the one in use stays.
