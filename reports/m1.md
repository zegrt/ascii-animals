  reading (a sample taken half a step before a step counts in it), 120 Hz leads 60 Hz by half a step: 48.6 % at the

### 1. The pointer's path: queued, timestamped, the same air at 15–120 Hz

- **What changed** (`Breeze.swift`, `MeadowTuning.Stir`):
  - The path is played back from each event's own time and place, `pathLag` behind (`PointerPlayback.played`).
  - A step whose place on the path isn't known yet (no sample at or after its time has arrived) now waits in a queue
    (up to 64 steps). It is played, in order, as soon as a sample arrives, stirring as that step would have, with
    its impulse decayed by `exp(−1.6 · dt)` for each step it waited. The field is linear, so late, paired and burst
    input end up as the on-time air. The old rule caught up in one step, which distorted speed and lost impulse at
    the clamp.
  - The breeze remembers its last 64 steps even while no way is playing. A way whose first samples arrive later
    than the lag still plays the steps due after its start: they no longer collapse into one stroke. I found this
    case with the new late-input check below.
  - Leaving and coming back while the way is still playing out no longer clears the queue. The way before the
    pointer left plays to its end, and the re-entry sample is marked as a jump, so nothing is stirred along the jump.
  - Samples more than 0.1 s apart are taken as the pointer resting, then moving over the last 0.1 s before the later
    one (`longestMove`), so a pause isn't smeared into a slow stroke.
  - `.sketch` keeps the sketch's `stirStep` exactly, and the replays use it.
- **The host's input contract** (documented on `Breeze`, for m7):
  - For every pointer event over the meadow, in order, call `pointerMoved(x:y:hostTime:)` with the event's own
    position and its own timestamp (`NSEvent.timestamp`), never the time it was delivered.
  - Call `pointerLeft()` when the pointer leaves the meadow.
  - Once a frame, after the frame's steps, call `syncInputClock(hostTime:speed:)` with the timestamp and speed
    `OceanClock.advance` got. The breeze mirrors the family clock's unstepped remainder and keeps the last 32 frames'
    anchors, so each event is placed through the frame it happened in, even when it is delivered a frame or a speed
    change later.
  - `pointerMoved(x:y:at:)` takes a time on the breeze's own input clock instead, for a host that keeps one.
- **The lag, re-judged.** This is the smallest lag that passes the full matrix, measured at every step and printed by
  the check:

  | lag | worst case in the matrix |
  |---|---|
  | 5/60 s | 42.5 % |
  | 6/60 s | 32.3 % |
  | 7/60 s | 20.5 % |
  | 8/60 s (0.133 s) | 9.9 % (fails) |
  | 8.01/60 s | 1.0 % (passes) |
  | **0.14 s (chosen)** | **0.9 %** |

  - The worst case is 15 Hz input in pairs. A step's place needs the sample after it, and of a pair the older
    sample arrives one sample interval plus a step late. So the path must play more than 8 steps behind.
  - 0.14 s is the smallest round value above that bound. The check holds the lag inside (8/60, 9/60) and requires
    8/60 to fail, so the lag can't drift up unnoticed.
  - It is 0.14 s more than the sketch's immediate response. See Concerns.
- **Evidence:**
  - `meadowStirSameAtEveryInputRate`, at every step, limit 5 %. 22 cases: two paths (the gesture, and the review's
    straight 540 pt/s sweep) at 15, 30, 60 and 120 Hz, regular, paired and jittered (0–3 steps late), each against

  - Bursts: 60 Hz in fours 0.0 %, 120 Hz in sixes 0.1 %.
  - The total air stirred at 30 Hz in pairs is within 1 % of the 60 Hz total.
  - For the record (not held): the sketch's own rule gives 10.8 % at 30 Hz and 33.3 % at 15 Hz. Unstamped 15 Hz

### Rulings (fix round 1)

1. **The lag is 0.14 s.** It is the smallest round value above the matrix's bound of 8 steps (0.133 s). If wrong, the
   pointer's air trails by 0.14 s. A smaller lag would need either a narrower matrix or predicting the path ahead, which
   overshoots when the pointer stops. From the same sweep: 30–120 Hz in every delivery passes at 6/60 s (0.1 s), and
   60–120 Hz at 5/60 s (0.083 s). The 0–50 ms jitter sets those, not the rate.
2. **The host check compares each case on its own frames and speeds.** At a speed change, `OceanClock` re-times the
   remainder it had accumulated, so step n falls at a slightly different real moment on different frame patterns
   (up to a step). Compared step for step, uneven frames with speed changes against even 60 Hz frames differ by up to
   9 %, and that comes from the clock, not the pointer. Across frame patterns at a steady speed the air is identical
   (0.0 %).
3. **Flowers record their screen**, a new `Flower.screen` field: the patch's screen, or the one clicked. Relayout
   judges each flower by the screen that holds it now, so a reorder of the screens doesn't close the meadow.
4. **`Flowers.relayout` lays out the breeze itself**, as the sketch's resize calls `pfInit`. If the host also calls
   `Breeze.layout`, the fields are reset twice, which is harmless.
5. **A replant keeps every fading flower** until its fade ends: planted ones displaced by the resize, and clicked ones
   off the screens. The sketch drops both at once.
6. **The probe's kinds** are made on a fresh stream, `mulberry32(777)`. The meadow's stream after the probe's steps

## Rulings

1. **The pointer's path is played back 0.1 s behind, from timed samples** (`Stir.pathLag`, the re-check's finding
   7).
   - How: `pointerMoved(x:y:at:)` records each sample with its time on the breeze's input clock (`dt / speed` a
     step). Each step, the stir point moves to where the path was 0.1 s ago, interpolated, and the sketch's
     `stirStep` stirs that segment. Samples later than the lag are caught up in one step, so no way is dropped.
   - Why: per-step averaging alone, which I tried first, still differed by 14–22 % for bursts, because only the
     samples' times can tell late input from fast input.
   - With no lag it is the sketch's rule exactly. The replays run it that way and match.
   - Cost if wrong: the pointer's air trails it by 0.1 s, and `setPathLag(0)` gives the sketch's rule back.
   - The host has to pass event times: unstamped bursts differ by 25.5 % (see Concerns).
2. **"Gap" and the 10 pt slack, per screen.**
   - A gap is a point inside the terrain's bounds that no screen holds.
   - A patch's blooms stand on its own screen, or up to 10 pt past one of its real edges on either axis, as the
     sketch's per-axis test does. They never stand past a shared edge onto the next screen, and never in a gap.
   - Each screen gets `max(1, round(…))` patches of its own; patch spacing counts every screen's centres.
   - With one screen this is exactly the sketch (the replays confirm it).
3. **Relayout fades more than the brief names.** Any clicked flower off the terrain fades, at any size of change,
   as the brief asks. I also fade a planted flower whose centre can no longer stand (off the screens, or now in a
   gap). In a big replant, a clicked flower off the screens fades instead of vanishing as in the sketch.
   - Cost if wrong: a planted flower near a moved edge fades rather than lingering off screen.
4. **The pollen sparks are drawn inside `Flowers`,** into a fixed 256-spark buffer that the world takes with
   `takeSparks`.
   - Why: a click's spark draws come between the flower's draws and the answer draw on the input stream, so drawing
     them later would shift the stream.
   - Consequence: the world (m6 and m7) must draw the click answer (`rnd() < 0.7`) after `click` returns. The puff's
     motion is m4's.
5. **A click off the terrain is ignored.** The sketch blooms wherever the canvas gets a click, but the port's
   clicks are only ever inside the screens (m7's rule). This is why the probes' click points are fractions of each
   viewport.
6. **A flower's reach uses `makeFlower`'s formula** (the longest leaf at full length). The sketch's `leaves` slider

   the painter (m4).
