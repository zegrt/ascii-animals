What `canvasGroundDraws` proves (GPU render, 4 x 3 cells at scale 2, grid on): a full ground under a block shows in
the block's left and top inset strip, and the grid line is the grid mixed over the ground, not over the background;
a ground under a glyph shows around the ink, and no pixel of that cell is the background; a half-alpha ground in an
empty cell gives background mixed with the ground by 128/255, across the cell and under its grid line; the other nine
cells are plain background; at reveal 0 every pixel is plain background; and on the next frame, after the painter
stops writing a ground, none is left (cleared and re-uploaded). `canvasGroundPut` covers starting empty (also after
`clear()`), growing on the first write, packing, out-of-range writes ignored, `put` leaving the ground alone,
`withGround`, and `clear()` resetting it.

Rulings:
- **The dissolve hides the ground.** A cell hidden by the dissolve draws the bare background, not its ground. The
  brief says the dissolve applies to the ground "exactly as to the background". I read that as keeping the existing
  contract: `encode`'s doc and `canvasComposeDraws` both say reveal 0 is bare background everywhere. The other way,
  the Meadow's green hints would appear all at once at the swap instead of dissolving in with the rest. If wrong, it
  is a one-line move of the ground read above the reveal early-return in `canvas_main`, plus the check's reveal-0
  part.
- **Ground mixes over the background by its alpha** (a = 1: the ground alone; a = 0: none). The sketch's ground is
  opaque, and alpha leaves room for soft shadow edges at no cost.
- **"Grow-only" means lazy, at both ends.** `ground` stays empty and no ground textures exist until a painter writes
  a ground. So Ocean and Web allocate, clear and upload nothing more, and their shader path is the old one (one
  uniform branch). Once grown it stays for the canvas's life (the canvas and textures are rebuilt only when the grid
  changes), so the Meadow allocates nothing per frame. `ground` is `private(set)`. Bulk writers use `withGround`,
  which makes sure the array has grown, so a painter can't write into an empty array. Cost if wrong: painters write
  through a closure instead of the array directly.
- **Light mode:** nothing to add. Canvas colours arrive final (no inversion in `canvas_main`), so the painter gives
  the paper ground, just as it gives the paper background.
- If making the ground textures fails, the frame draws without a ground and tries again next frame. The existing
  canvas textures behave the same way.

Noticed:
- `OceanTool`'s `Digest.add(_ c: CellCanvas)` (Bench.swift) doesn't hash `c.ground`. That is right for Web, whose
  ground is always empty; adding an empty array leaves its digests unchanged. When the Meadow joins the digest (m8),
  add `c.ground` there. That file is outside m3's Files.
- `bench --digest` covers only Web's CPU canvas. For Ocean, the GPU stills above and `oceanStillUnchanged` are the
  proof, since Ocean never uses `canvas_main` (it draws through `compose_main`).
