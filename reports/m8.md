## The decision needed

Two of the director's decisions clash, and `meadowWithinWebsCPU` fails because of it:

- d1, approved: "7 is the laptop number; bigger displays get more by area" (the sketch's `areaCount`, 1440 × 900
  reference, at most 24 per screen).
- The port plan: "the Meadow must cost no more CPU than Web at 30 and 15 fps".

On the owner's desk (2560 × 1440 above 1800 × 1169, the check's desk) area scaling gives 20 + 11 = **31
butterflies**. Measured with the check, same machine, same build:

| | Meadow, 30 fps | Web, 30 fps |
|---|---|---|
| area scaling off (7 butterflies, as mp shipped) | 10.71 ms/s (15 fps: 5.84) | 15.55 ms/s (15 fps: 12.39) |
| area scaling on (31 butterflies, this branch) | 27.81 ms/s | 16.09 ms/s |

About 0.7 ms/s per extra butterfly at 30 fps, so Web's budget holds about 14 on that desk. I left the scaling on as
the approved design says and did not touch the check. Options for the director:

1. **Cap the scene** (e.g. at most 12 per scene, the slider's top): the desk gets 12, a laptop still 7, a single big
   monitor 12 instead of 20. Within budget. One constant.
2. **Keep the design's scaling and relax the CPU rule** for counts the person chose (the budget then holds for the
   default on a laptop, not on a big desk).
3. **No area scaling**: 7 everywhere ("How many" means "on screen"); the footnote under How many changes.

My recommendation: 1 (keeps "more on a bigger display" while honouring the CPU rule; the scene still feels as dense
as the laptop on a single large monitor up to about 1.7× a laptop's area).

A second, smaller point for the same answer: the design's reference is 1440 × 900, but today's built-in displays at
their default scaling are a bit bigger, so "7 on a laptop" shows 8 on a 13" Air (1470 × 956) or 14" Pro
(1512 × 982), and 10 on a 15" Air or 16" Pro (1710 × 1112, 1728 × 1117). If the director wants exactly 7 on their
laptop, the reference (`MeadowTuning.areaCountReference`) should be their laptop's size.

### Concerns

- **The 30 fps margin is thin.** At 12 butterflies on the owner's desk the Meadow costs about 92–97% of Web at

- Each screen's count is still rounded before the scene's total is capped, as in round 1, so below the cap every

   most 1. The rule is unchanged (Meadow no more than Web). It prints both habitats' median ms/s, the median ratio
   and the range of the ratios. It takes about 9 s.

   virtual machines on shared hosts; thread CPU time is still measured there, and since each pair runs back to
   back on the same runner and the verdict is the median ratio, a slow or busy runner raises both sides of each
   pair alike. A runner that gets much slower for a moment can spoil one or two pairs; it would take five of the
   nine to flip the result. The QoS hint does nothing on a VM's virtual cores, which is fine: they are all one
   kind.
