## Design exercise (the director's "think it through" first)

### How a butterfly reads through a wingbeat and at rest

- The wings beat about the body's long axis. Seen from above or behind (how the app draws its open poses, the
  pinned-specimen view), a wing's visible width is cos(stroke): widest level on the downstroke, narrower on the
  upstroke, and only an edge at the clap, when the two pairs meet over the back. Seen from the side it is the
  other way round: at the clap the pressed-together pair faces you and shows its full shape, and the face you see
  is the underside.
- A real viewer is almost never exactly above. From a little above and to one side, every beat shows the upper side
  opening and then the underside as the wings close. This is the morpho's blue-and-brown flash. The clap is the
  moment of the underside, not of nothing.
- At rest (nectaring, basking closed, roosting), the wings stay pressed together over the back for 15–60 s. From
  the side you see one solid wing pair in its underside colours: the forewing's apex up and forward, the rounded
  hindwing below it, the swallowtail's tail hanging off it, the body along the wings' root and the antennae
  forward. The far pair is hidden directly behind it. People recognise a perched butterfly by this sail.

### What reads at 13 × 19 pt cells in this app's style

- Area first. A shape reads as a thing once it is about 3 or more columns of solid blocks wide. A one-column
  feature reads as a line, and in the meadow lines are stems and grass. The rejected clap and closed poses were
  lines (`| / \` contours), so they read as plants or nothing.
- Then the signature. A dark body column with clubbed antennae standing clear above it reads as "butterfly". A
  forewing-over-hindwing outline with a notch keeps it from reading as a leaf or a moth.
- Then the marks, and only inside an area that already reads. Dark-block veins and borders inside a narrow wing
  cut it into pieces (the review's "loose contours"). On a small or folded wing, marks have to sit on the face's
  colour (glyphs on its block), not replace it.
- Flicker sets a limit too. A cell may go empty↔full at most twice in a beat. Any width that dips and recovers
  inside a beat costs extra switches.

### Three ways to draw the clap and the closed pose

```
A · held V (both pairs, narrowed      B · profile sail (one pair beside      C · folded lozenge over the body
    to a minimum width, underside)        the body, underside; far pair          (both pairs pressed, seen
                                          tucked behind)                         face on)
      °   °                                °  °                                  °   °
       \ /                                  \/   ####                             \ /
   ##%#o#%##                                o  ######°                          .#####.
    ##:8:##                                 8 ##-###-##                         #######
    #° 8 °#                                 8 #########                         ##@@@##
     ## | ##                                | ###\\###                          #######
     ## : ##                                :  ####° %                           #####
                                                ##                                ###
```

- **A, held V:** symmetric, so it always reads as a butterfly, never a stick. Its width is held (not dipped and
  re-grown), so it adds no empty↔full switches to the beat. It does not look closed, though: it looks like a
  smaller butterfly in its underside colours.
- **B, profile sail:** the most recognisable resting butterfly, exactly "one solid shape the size of one wing pair"
  in its underside colours. It can't be used for the clap in flight: the near wing would go full → narrow → full
  sail → narrow → full in every beat, four switches, which breaks the flicker rule.
- **C, lozenge:** hides the body column and the antenna roots. At this cell size it reads as a leaf or a petal.
  Rejected.

**Chosen: A for the clap and B for rest, joined by the stroke.** From a 45° to a 62° stroke each wing's drawn width
is held up to 0.45 of open, and past 68° it shows its underside. That covers the clap in flight and the flash. From
84° to 89° the pair folds into B: the near pair widens to a full wing pair in underside colours and the far pair
tucks behind. The flight's stroke tops out at 80° (strokeMid + strokeAmp for every species), so the fold happens
only at rest. A pose with exactly one banked wing edge-on still draws that wing as a line, as `edgeOnIsALine`
requires and as the re-check accepted for the hard-bank pose.

## What was built

   width, face and edge-on flag, from the rule above. Underside patterns per species follow research §5.4 and the
   brief:

   - morpho: brown with a row of four ringed eyespots, which shrink with the wing's width;

   Underside veins and bars are line glyphs on the face's block (`LINE`), not dark blocks, so the folded wing stays

   Targets near the sides or top are aimed where the drawing fits (`aimPoint`). The ground still gives way to a
   low flower, as before. Waypoints sit at the drawing's reach. Tiny screens keep the quarter-of-the-short-side cap,

3. **Finding 7:** the ground cache's per-plant key now holds the base's y and the rows the plant draws over
   (`r0`, `r1`). `changed` is split into typed parts for CI.
4. **No allocation when butterflies are added:** the per-butterfly buffers are sized up front for 16 (or the flock
   if bigger), each the biggest a species grows to (size 1 + spread) at this cell.

## Rulings

These are small calls I made alone, each with what it costs if wrong.

- **Per-axis margins from the drawing's actual reach**, not the half-span everywhere. The half-span vertically
  (143–189 pt against about 99 up and 93–127 down) squeezed vertical roaming, so `freeFlightCoversTheField` failed
  on height. The brief's purpose, no drawing off the screen, is checked with these reaches. If wrong, the top and
  bottom margins grow and coverage needs another fix.
- **A hard stop at the reach behind the soft margin.** A soft steer alone let butterflies, and seekers pulled by a
  target near a wall, cross the reach by up to about 20 pt. If wrong: when the soft steer hasn't turned it, a
  butterfly slides along an edge for a moment. `flightPathsMatchMeasurements` still passes; the white's a_n / a_t
  over seeds 88–92 is 0.95, 0.80, 1.07, 0.69 and 1.24 (bounds 0.55–1.25). Seed 92 was 1.14 before this change and
  sits near the bound.
- **Three changes to the roaming, all for coverage** (`freeFlightCoversTheField` failed with the big morpho on the
  1800 × 1169 field). These change t1-2's free-flight character a little:
  - waypoints placed at the drawing's reach;
  - a waypoint counts as reached within 0.5 wingspan (was 1);
  - the waypoint score weighs "going somewhere" 1.0 (was 0.5).

  Over the check's 32 runs, the narrowest track spans 0.732 of the width (needs 0.7) and the shortest 0.674 of the
  height (needs 0.6). Before this round the narrowest was 0.766 width. The margin to the threshold is small for the
  morpho.
- **Seekers' targets near the sides and the top are moved in** to where the drawing fits. A flower within a drawn
  half-span of a side edge can't be reached, only hovered beside.
- **Clap and fold numbers:** the width is held at 0.45, the underside shows past 68°, and the fold runs from 84° to
  89°. The fold's near side follows the bank's sign, else the heading.
- **Underside veins are glyphs on the face's block**, not dark blocks, so the folded shape stays solid.
- **The cabbage white's underside block on paper** went from (214, 214, 196) to (208, 212, 168): it was nearly the
  upper side's colour.
- **The morpho's eyespot ring block on paper** is now tinted, so its `o` shows.
- **The meadow-still size grammar:** digits with at most one point. A leading `+` is refused too.

## What was built

### Item 1: nothing drawn leaves the screen

**The check comes first.** `nothingDrawnLeavesTheScreen` paints real flights through the painter onto a canvas padded
all round, on each screen's own grid. It compares every drawn glyph, block and overlay cell with the screen's whole
cells: a cell outside the screen, or in its last partial column or row, counts as off it. It covers:
- one butterfly of each species flying freely on 340 × 150 (the preview's 8-pt cell) with the weather on, from the
  middle (seed 7, as the review's probe) and from random starts, one of them at the biggest size, 60 s each;
- pursuits on 2560 × 1440 and 1440 × 900 (each at its own cell) of a one-row head in the middle and near the left,
  and of heads at the left edge (x 7 and x 39), the right edge and the top (y 1), from the middle of the screen, in
  calm air and in weather, 30 s each;
- banked starts at the left, right, top and bottom edges and two corners, facing out of the screen. The first step
  crosses the wall, as the review's morpho at (1, 720) did.

**The fix:**
- `Butterfly.drawnExtent` (in `MeadowPainter.swift`, next to the outlines it reads) gives the drawing's reach left,
  right, up and down. It uses the painter's own geometry:
  - both wing outlines at any width from 0 to open;
  - the edge-on lines, the body, the proboscis, and the antennae and clubs with their sway;
  - the body's lean and the tips' climb;
  - the samples' quarter-cell soft edge.

  Every cell the painter draws holds a point inside that reach.
- The hard stop uses it (never less than the old upright reach, which `drawingStaysOnScreen` holds). It also keeps
  the drawing out of a screen's last partial column and row (each wall's own rect, its grid at the rect's corner).
  A step that left the terrain is brought back inside first; one flying in from outside is left alone, for t4's
  crossings.
- **A low flower:** the flight's point still goes down to the head (the ground gives way to a target, as before). The
  body is drawn above it by what the point lacks of the drawing's room (`drawnOffset`). The flight's point is then
  the mouth on the head, and the drawing stays on the screen. `flowerSeekersReachLowHeads` still passes, and
  `lowFlowersKeepTheirReach` holds the same reach on both screens at their own cells.
- **The preview:** where the walls' margins are capped (a quarter of the short side), the flight keeps to its capped
  soft walls, as the brief says, and is not stopped. The body is drawn where the whole drawing fits (`drawnOffset`
  again).

  A drawing that cannot fit the screen's whole cells at any lean is drawn at the share of its size that does
  (`fit`). This happens only on the preview: at 340 × 150, the white is drawn at 1, the monarch at 0.88 (0.74 at its
  biggest), the morpho at 0.78 (0.67) and the swallowtail at 0.67 (0.58).
- **The lean near walls:** the drawn lean eases to upright within 0.3 wingspan of where a wall stops the upright
  drawing (`leanRoom`). Without it, the lean (up to ±29°) lifts the far wing tip about 27 pt and pushes the leading tip
  out just as a butterfly meets a wall, and free flight lost its roaming (`freeFlightCoversTheField` failed; see
  Rulings).

- At 75° and at the species' own flight peak (mid + amplitude: 80°, 80°, 75°, 80°), the near side holds at least five

  nearer the underside's than the upper side's.
- From 50° to the peak in 1° steps, each block is sorted by which face's colour it is nearer. No step turns more than

- At rest (84–89°) the near pair opens to the full sail, as before. The open, downstroke and hard-bank poses are

## Rulings

These are small calls I made alone, each with what it costs if wrong.

- **The flight's point is the mouth at a low flower**, and the body is drawn above it (`drawnOffset`). This was the
  only way to keep both the brief's "the mouth still reaches the head" and t1-2's `flowerSeekersReachLowHeads`,
  which measures the flight's point. If wrong, t3 must define the mouth differently and this check changes owner.
  t3's landing should use `drawnOffset` for where the body and proboscis are drawn.
- **On the preview the drawn body is placed, the flight isn't stopped.** Stopping the flight at the drawing's bounds
  there pins it to the middle third: the antennae alone reach about 40 pt above the body on 12-pt rows, so
  `freeFlightRoamsATinyScreen` can't pass. What it costs: on the preview, the drawn body sits up to about 72 pt from
  the flight's point, so the drawing roams less than the flight.
- **`fit`: drawings too big for a screen at any lean are drawn smaller.** On 340 × 150 the swallowtail can't fit
  12 whole rows even upright at its biggest. Only the preview is affected (fit is 1 at 1440 × 900 and up). If wrong,
  the preview needs fewer rows of drawing, for example shorter antennae on 8-pt cells.
- **The drawn lean eases to upright within 0.3 wingspan of a wall.** Without it the true leaning reach costs the
  free flight 0.02–0.04 of its roaming. What it costs: butterflies near an edge are drawn upright. 0.5 wingspan
  measured the same; 0.3 changes the look least.
- **Clap numbers:**
  - hold 0.72 from 42.5° (0.7 left the swallowtail's clap at 41 blocks against the third of 124 that
    `closedWingsShowTheUnderside` needs);
  - tuck 60–72°;
  - underside sweep 58–73°, from the tips in.
- **"Whole cells" means the screen's last partial column and row are off it.** This is what the review counted at
  1440 × 900. It costs up to one cell at the right and bottom edges.
