What each finding became:
1. **Presentation.** No more `asyncAfter` chains: a waiting card looks once a second (`WhatsNew.Presenter`). Each look
   reads the lock, console and screensaver from the OS (`SystemSignals.sessionTruth`, not only the notification
   state) and the chosen display's sleep with `CGDisplayIsAsleep`, so a display already asleep at launch counts. Clear
   at the first look (a second after launch), it opens at once; after any wait it opens only once the screen has
   stayed clear for a full second, so a wake that relocks spends nothing. The look that opens it is the latest
   word: the card is built, ordered forward, and kept only if the panel is visible on a screen and that screen is
   still seeable; only then is `whatsNew.meadow.v1` saved. Otherwise it goes at once, unmarked, and waits again. No
   screens: it waits (no array index; `target` returns nil).
2. **Help's welcome.** `WhatsNewCard.dismiss()` stops the looks and closes a card up at once (no fade under the
   welcome). Every look re-reads the saved flag and waits while a welcome is up. If a card was still waiting and the
   welcome couldn't open (no renderer, no screens), it waits again; a welcome that reached the screen saves the flag,
   so the card never follows it.
3. **Esc and clicks.** Esc closes the card only while ASCII Animals is active (`NSApp.isActive`) and the event is
   returned, so it still reaches Settings or a sheet. From the moment closing starts, the panel ignores mouse events.
4. **The preview.** `WhatsNew.preview` follows the desktop's own rule (`PowerPolicy.mode`) except that a paused desktop
   or covered windows don't stop it: live; a still in Low Power Mode (live if "keep animating" is on) or when too hot;
   nothing drawn when locked, asleep, behind the screensaver or in another session. Every power change reaches the
   card (`signals.onChange`). The live view joins the card the first time it goes live and then stays (moving an
   AppKit view in and out of SwiftUI's tree is fragile); its display link is started and stopped, and the still or a
   plain plate covers it. A card opened in Low Power Mode never lays out or warms the live view.
5. **Upgrader without the tour mark.** `launchWelcome`: not onboarded gives the tour only for a fresh install
   (`since31`); an upgrade gets the Meadow card, with the Sharks Web card still first.
6. **Placement.** `WhatsNew.anchor` uses the icon only when it is a real frame on a screen and in that screen's menu
   bar (at or above the visible frame), and the app's `iconAnchorFrame` also needs the icon's window to be showing;
   anything else is the top-right fallback with no arrow, on the main display. The card's width is the design's 460
   pt or what fits with 8 pt each side (`WhatsNew.width`); the frame is kept inside the visible frame either way. On
   a narrow card the chips keep their words and the "esc close" hint gives way (`ViewThatFits`).
7. **The still's framing.** The still is cut from a meadow 2.4 previews wide and 4 tall, laid out and settled as
   `StillRenderer` does, at the live preview's own cell (8 pt; the area stays under the 1,054 pt where the cell would
   grow). `WhatsNew.frame` picks a crop on a whole butterfly (its painter's half-span and body length turned to its
   heading) with its wings at least 60 % open, clear of the blooms unless it has landed on one in bold colours (never
   a white on a bloom), with most of a bloom in it; it then prefers no other butterfly cut, few blooms cut, bold kinds
   and size. The best of 96 seeds wins; all three line-ups take 0.01 s in release.
8. **Keep chip.** A habitat change made elsewhere relabels it ("Keep the Web"); the Meadow picked elsewhere closes the
   card, as before.
