## How this differs from plan Task 5, because there is no MeadowWorld yet
- The painter's input is `paint(land: Meadowland, butterflies: [Butterfly], breeze: Breeze, time: Double,
  viewport: CGRect, grid: GridSpec, theme: ThemeParams, into canvas: CellCanvas)`. t3 will wrap it for its world.
- The life-story drawing (caterpillar, chrysalis, the empty case) is NOT in this task; it moves to t4.
- The GPU check `meadowComposeDraws` goes through `encode(habitat:)` using a small test-only `CellPainter` wrapper
  inside OceanChecks (land + butterflies + breeze + this painter), as 3.0 did before its habitat existed.
- Add `OceanTool meadow-still <out.png> <W>x<H> [--light] [--calm] [--seconds S] [--seed N]`: lay out the land on one
  rect, fly a few of each species with `Butterfly.fly` toward random flowers for S seconds (default 20), then paint
  one frame and write the PNG. Set each butterfly's `scale` from the cell (`cell.width / 13`).

### The look (the director's ask, and review findings 1 and 3)
- **Bigger butterflies:** about 1.7 × today's size on screen. Change the species sizes in
  `ButterflySpecies.swift` (span × height in cells: roughly monarch 22 × 9, morpho 26 × 9, swallowtail 22 × 9 plus
  tails, cabbage white 15 × 7). Change nothing else in the flight model; t1-2's checks must still pass.
- **Read as butterflies at a glance**, in open, banked and closed poses: two clear wing pairs (forewing and
  hindwing contours), a visible gap or dark body column down the middle, antennae standing out above the head,
  wings filled mostly with solid colour blocks with pattern marks only where the real species has them (monarch
  veins and white margin dots, the morpho's shine and brown underside, the swallowtail's bars, blue and red spots
  and tails joined to the hindwings, the white's black tips and dots). Fewer filler letters.
- Use the model's signed wing widths (finding 3) where it reads well; where the physical width would flicker, say in
  the report what you chose and why.
- **Better flowers:** fuller, bigger, more colourful heads, 2–3 rows tall and 3–7 columns wide by kind (research
  §4.1 shapes: a daisy's white petal ring round a gold eye, a coneflower's drooping pink petals round a dark cone,
  milkweed's pink ball, fennel's flat yellow umbel, clover's round head), leaves on the stems, and plants in clumps
  so the strip reads as a meadow, not a row of single stems. Fewer bare spindly tall stems. Head sizes and layout
  live in `Flowers.swift`; drawing in the painter.
- Compare with the app's sharks and spiders at the same size (`OceanTool` can render Ocean and Web stills): the
  butterflies should be at least as readable.
