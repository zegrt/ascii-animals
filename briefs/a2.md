# Brief a2: performance across the app, deeper (an audit, read-only)

Size: Review (one auditor; no code changes)
Board commit: the commit that adds this brief (main)
Code: `meadow-t11` @ 971a216 (the whole 3.1 stack: Ocean, Web, Meadow, the app shell). Write `reviews/a2.md` on a new
branch `review-a2` made from main; commit only.

## Why
The director: "find more and deeper and more extensive perf improvements". 3.1 already has one performance pass (pf:
the Meadow's simulation, byte-for-byte equal output) and a live energy session (t8, board row and `docs/DEVELOPING.md`,
"All three habitats, 3.1"). What is known so far, as facts to check rather than take as given:
- On the owner's desk (built-in Retina 1800 × 1169 pt + a 2560 × 1440 monitor at 100 Hz, count 7), the Meadow costs
  the app the least CPU of the three habitats, but WindowServer runs about 3 points higher for it at 30 fps than for
  the Ocean and the Web. The working guess is the Meadow's per-cell ground being uploaded every frame; nobody has
  proven it.
- The a1 audit (`git show origin/review-a1:reviews/a1.md`, findings 7 and 9) found GPU backpressure that can starve UI
  work, frame submission treated as presentation, and render cadence, elapsed time and simulation pacing coupled.
  Those are routed to 3.1.1; go deeper on their cost side, don't just repeat them.
- Builders are changing these files right now: r1 (`PointerMonitor`, `PointerRouter`, `OceanDirector` pointer entry
  points), r2 (wallpaper code, `main.swift`), r3 (updates, settings, diagnostics), t11 (the what's-new card, the
  welcomes, `LiveOceanView`). Read them as they are at 971a216; say where a finding touches one of them.

## Do
Find where the app spends CPU, GPU, memory, WindowServer compositing and wake-ups, in every habitat and every
state, and what would cut each one. Cover at least:
1. **The frame path end to end:** simulation, glyph and cell building, buffer uploads (what changes per frame
   versus what is re-sent), Metal encoding, drawable acquisition, presentation, compositing in WindowServer (layer
   opacity, the window's opaque flag, contents scale, pixel formats, sizes per display, damaged-area versus full-frame
   redraws). Explain the Meadow's extra WindowServer cost from the code, and say how to prove it.
2. **Idle and hidden states:** covered by full-screen apps, another Space, the screen asleep, locked, the screensaver,
   Low Power Mode, on battery, Calm. What still runs (timers, display links, simulation, the menu bar, the reading
   timer), how often it wakes the CPU, and what should stop entirely.
3. **Several displays:** per-display work that could be shared or skipped, refresh rates that differ (the 100 Hz
   monitor settles at 33.3 fps), mirrored displays, a display with nothing visible on it.
4. **Memory and allocation:** footprint per display (frame buffers, textures, atlases), steady-state allocation per
   frame, caches that grow, leaks across display changes, sleep and habitat switches.
5. **Start-up and switching:** launch time to the first frame, switching habitats and presets, the Settings and
   About windows and the what's-new card's own live preview (do they render a second habitat while open?).
6. **The details:** per-frame string or dictionary work, `Date()` or `ProcessInfo` calls in hot loops, logging on hot
   paths, `DispatchQueue` hops, retain/release traffic, Swift generics or existentials in inner loops, unnecessary
   `CGImage` or `NSImage` conversions, anything that runs per cell that could run per row or per change.

Measure what you can **offscreen**: `swift run -c release OceanTool bench` (and `--desk`, `--habitat web|meadow`),
`swift run -c release OceanChecks`, `xcrun xctrace` or `sample` **only against OceanTool runs**, timing and
allocation counts from your own throwaway copies of the bench (in a detached worktree, never committed). Say plainly
which numbers are measured and which are estimates.

For each finding: what costs what (measured, or estimated with your reasoning), every place in the code (file:line),
the change that cuts it at the cause, the expected gain on the owner's desk, the risk (visible change? the owner tuned
the Meadow's look by eye, so any change to pixels needs the director), how to prove it (an equivalence check like pf's
byte-for-byte frames, a bench, or a live `Tools/energy.sh` run with the director), and its size.

Rank by gain for users on a normal day (a laptop on battery, one or two displays, the wallpaper mostly covered by
windows). End with a short list: the improvements in the order to make them, grouped so each group's files don't
overlap the others' or the open tasks above, so the manager can brief builders.
