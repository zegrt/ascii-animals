## The director's verdict on the first film (2026-11-20) — this shapes everything below
"1. to many 2. they gather around to mcuh and look like they roam around the same places and not enough
movevemt". The realism line follows from it: movement and spread across the whole screen come first; real
behaviours stay, but tuned so the meadow is always moving. Concretely:
- **Fewer butterflies.** Use the plan's density (1 per 0.4 Mpt² × density, about 3 on a laptop, 9 on a 2560 ×
  1440 monitor, cap 16), and make the default density setting produce that. The film had 8 on a laptop: too many.
- **Spread out.** Butterflies keep their distance from each other (a separation rule, like the sharks': about
  three wingspans, stronger when both are slow), at most one butterfly per flower, and a butterfly picks flowers
  away from others. Courtship chases (t4) are the only time two come close.
- **Roam the whole screen, not the same places.** Long travelling flights across the screen, upper half included;
  waypoints spread out and avoid where the butterfly has recently been (a short memory of recent places). Reduce
  the free flight's bottom bias (`Butterfly.lowWaypoints`, now 0.65) so the upper screen gets real use; flowers
  still draw them down when they're hungry.
- **More movement.** At any moment most butterflies are flying. Nectar and bask stays use the short end of the
  research ranges (for example a few seconds to about 15 s), and a butterfly between flowers travels rather than
  hovering near the last one.
- **Check it on film, not only with checks:** end the task with a 30 s real-speed film (`meadow-frames`, now flying
  a `MeadowWorld`) at 1440 × 900 and 2560 × 1440, watched and described in the report. Add checks for the
  intent: on a laptop at default density, at any sampled moment at least two thirds of the butterflies are flying,
  no two are within two wingspans for long (except courting), and over 2 minutes each butterfly visits at least
  6 of 9 screen cells.

## Carried over from earlier tasks (board routing)
- A clicked flower's index can be reused for a new flower after 40 clicks per rect (t1-2 review, finding 11). The
  world must re-validate a butterfly's flower before it lands or nectars (for example a generation counter), and a
  butterfly whose flower changed picks again.
- The world sets every `Butterfly.scale` from the cell (`cell.width / 13`) at layout (t1-2 finding 7).
- `Meadowland.bloom(time:)` must be given the land's own clock (t1-2 fix round, finding 8).
- The pointer's breeze trail (t5 review, finding 8): the painter can't tell the pointer's air from the weather,
  because `Breeze` sums them. Keep the pointer's contribution apart in `Breeze` (for example a second, short-lived
  field written by `write`), and have the painter brighten stems, grass and a few trail cells from it only, never
  from gusts. Add a check: a pointer stroke through open air leaves trail cells; a weather gust alone leaves none.
- Perching (t5 restart 2): a feeding or resting butterfly perches on its flower: feet and mouth at the head, body
  above it, wings as the pose says; the proboscis is drawn into the head. Butterflies may flutter partly off
  screen (board decision of 2026-11-20); only the body stays on a screen, so flowers near the edges stay
  reachable. Add a check: a butterfly nectaring on a one-row flower has its drawn proboscis on the head's cell.
- t5's painter takes `(land, butterflies, breeze, time, …)`. Add a convenience `paint(_ w: MeadowWorld, viewport:,
  grid:, theme:, into:)` that calls it, and switch `OceanTool meadow-still` to fly a `MeadowWorld` instead of its
  hand-flown butterflies, and the same for `OceanTool meadow-frames`.
- `meadow-frames` fixes from t5v's review (`git show review-t5v:reviews/t5v.md`): refuse an output folder that
  already holds frames (no deleting, no stale tails); `--seconds 0` and the fps list behave as for the still.
