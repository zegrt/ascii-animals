## Objective
The t8 session ran with the director on 2026-11-21 (board, t8 row). Energy is measured and valid. One thing failed:
**the what's-new card draws empty on the real desktop.** `Tools/energy.sh preview` launched the test copy, the copy
logged `energy-card: ... shown`, and the window server listed the card's panel on screen (layer 25 = `.statusBar`,
alpha 1, 460 × 333 pt at x 1036, y 41 on the built-in display, 1800 × 1169 pt), yet the director saw nothing there,
twice, and their screenshot of the built-in display shows only the test copy's Ocean wallpaper in that area. Until
now the card was only ever seen in offscreen snapshots (t9's probe), where it draws correctly. Find why the live
card draws nothing, fix it, and leave evidence that it draws, so the director's next look succeeds.

## Do
1. **The cause.** Read the card's code path from `open` to `arm` and find why nothing is drawn live. Likely
   suspects to rule in or out with evidence, not guesswork: the view tree is laid out at zero size or never drawn in
   a non-key, non-activating panel; `contentVisible` (or the typing) never advances outside an active app or a run
   loop mode; the fade's `animator()` never applies to the content; the SwiftUI hosting view never gets a display
   pass; the live preview view covers the card with nothing. Reproduce it on this Mac with the **test copy only**
   (`Tools/build.sh --test`, then run the bundle's executable with a `--snapshot-*` flag or a probe you add; never the
   director's app, never without `ASCII_ANIMALS_NO_HANDOVER=1`, never touching `~/Applications` or the real defaults).
   A live, on-screen launch of the test copy needs the director: if you can't establish the cause without one, stop
   and tell the manager exactly what you'd run.
2. **The fix**, in the card's code, the smallest that makes the live card draw, keeping t9's rules (never takes the
   keyboard, Esc only while the app is active, a click elsewhere closes it, once per upgrade, waits for a seeable
   screen).
3. **Evidence it draws, without screen capture.** In preview mode, after the fade-in, the test copy renders the
   card's own content view into a bitmap from inside the app (e.g. `bitmapImageRepForCachingDisplay` +
   `cacheDisplay`, or the layer tree) and logs, bound to its launch id, the share of non-transparent pixels and the
   view's size: `energy-card: pid=<p> launch=<id> drawn=<share> size=<w>x<h>`. `energy.sh preview` waits for that
   line and fails when the share is near zero, before asking the director. Show in the report that this check
   **fails on the current code** (8d310ca) and passes with the fix, the same way it would live. Save that bitmap as
   a PNG in `build/energy-test` for the manager to look at.
4. **Calibration's reading check (from the session):** the test copy's energy reading timer stops while one of its
   menus is open (a 14 s gap), and `calibrate`'s last `check_readings` covers the whole launch, so the gap failed a
   correct calibration. Schedule the reading timer so menus don't pause it (`.common` run-loop mode) and have
   calibration's last check cover only the readings after the director's answer. Measurement runs keep the 7 s rule.
5. **The energy numbers in the docs:** a 3.1 table in `docs/DEVELOPING.md` next to the 3.0 two-display table (same
   columns and style), from `build/energy-test/results.log` as the board's t8 row quotes it: built-in + BenQ
   2560 × 1440 at 100 Hz (so the external runs at 33.3 and 16.7 fps), plugged in, Automatic, 60 s per row after 10 s
   of settling, count 7, baseline WindowServer 8.9 % before and 6.9 % after. State plainly: the Meadow costs the app
   the least of the three; WindowServer is about 3 points higher for it at 30 fps. Update the README's battery note
   to cover the Meadow in the same plain words as its Ocean and Web sentences (no new claims).
6. **The runbook** (`reports/t8.md`): the display line is the BenQ, `calibrate` and `preview` need a real terminal
   (`/dev/tty`), and the card's new drawn check. Add a "Session, 2026-11-21" section with the results and what failed.
