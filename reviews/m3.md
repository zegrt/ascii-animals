# Review m3: a ground colour per cell in the shared canvas

Brief: briefs/m3.md      Reviewed commit: 2640dc6d7a40711f0bda0811ed11e135639b874f

## Design

Changes requested for the ground upload path. Ocean and Web passed the visual parity gate.

- The canvas stores one row-major RGBA8 texel per cell. Alpha zero selects the uniform background. The upload row pitch and the shader coordinates match the block texture. The shader mixes ground before the grid, the block inset 1 pt from the top and left, and both glyph layers. The 64-byte uniform layout is unchanged. The painter supplies the finished palette, including the paper colours and Calm dimming toward the base, as it does for other canvas colours.
- The dissolve ruling holds. Hidden cells show the uniform background, without grid, block, glyph or ground. This preserves the existing reveal contract and lets the Meadow's tint and shadows reveal with their cells. Showing the ground while those cells are hidden would expose the incoming Meadow ground across the screen at the habitat swap.
- Ground textures use the existing three in-flight slots. On a successful resize the renderer waits for submitted work, then discards the old canvas and texture ring. An offscreen Metal probe queued frames with distinct ground colours and checked their readbacks, then checked a resize from a 4×3 grid at 2× to an 8×3 grid at 1.5×, including the clipped bottom-right cell, followed by a scale-only resize. A light/Calm frame with reveal 0.3 showed the finished ground in a revealed cell and the paper background in its hidden neighbour. Switching the same viewport from the ground painter to real Web and Ocean simulations reproduced their fresh BGRA bytes.

Independent checks ran on detached worktrees at main `cb7bdf8` and the reviewed commit.

- `swift build` passed on both. `swift run -c release OceanChecks` ran with Metal enabled and reported 181 passed on main and 183 on m3, with zero failures. Both passed `oceanStillUnchanged`, `webMathMatchesOriginal`, `webWorldMatchesOriginal`, `webSettleIsTheOriginalStill` and `webGlyphsMatchChrome`.
- `swift run -c release OceanTool bench --digest` produced identical output on both commits. The 900-frame shared Web run produced scene digest `b9377f781b78cb6c` and total `612a0c22c778b40b`. With `--separate`, the two scenes produced `bf4a8b4851f303e0` and `37a29d2f57e87a67`, with total `d4d23789aedfab08`. The digest command always constructs a Web simulation (`Sources/OceanTool/main.swift:122`, `Sources/OceanTool/Bench.swift:119`).
- `OceanTool still` produced eight byte-identical PNG pairs. Each habitat was rendered at 960×541 points, 2×, seed 3, in default dark, light and Calm, and at 721×437 points, 1.5×, seed 5, in live light/Calm. Showing ground through hidden cells failed the reveal-0 assertion. Skipping the ground reset failed both `canvasGroundPut` and the GPU check for visible ground after clearing.

## What is missing?

The checked-in GPU test stops after one ground-free frame and uses a fixed grid. Extend it through reuse of previously populated ring slots and across a resize. Verify that frames with no ground writes skip the upload, since pixel checks alone can pass while the renderer transfers a full texture of zeros.

Local Swift 6.4 type-checked OceanKit and OceanChecks without 100 ms expression warnings. OceanChecks also emitted no 250 ms function-body warnings. The new renderer SIMD expressions have explicit types. Swift 6.3.3 was unavailable locally, so the CI compile remains unverified.
